# Pretrained Backbones Research v2 - RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection

Follow-up to `pretrained_medical_backbones_research.md` (v1). This version goes beyond v1: it verifies v1's "unreleased" claims against the current state (September 2026), and adds several models v1 missed entirely.

Abbreviations used throughout: Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic curve (AUROC), Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) - a way of training a model on unlabeled images by having it predict something about the image itself (e.g. matching two crops of the same picture) rather than a human-provided label, Vision Transformer (ViT) - an image model architecture that splits an image into small square patches and processes them the way a language model processes words, Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), Computed Tomography (CT) - a different scan type that uses X-rays, not magnetism, Musculoskeletal (MSK) - relating to bones, joints, muscles, tendons, ligaments, Chest X-ray (CXR).

## 1. Headline

The honest state of the art has not changed in kind since v1, only in detail: there is still no knee-MRI-pretrained 2D backbone you can download today that beats your ImageNet ConvNeXt-Base solo. What did change: a genuinely new, easy, ungated, correctly-licensed candidate surfaced (RadioDINO, self-supervised DINO training on the RadImageNet corpus, loadable with one line of `timm.create_model`), and DINOv3's license was clarified to permit commercial/competition use (with an attribution string), making DINOv3 ConvNeXt a real, nearly free experiment since it drops into your existing ConvNeXt code path. Every "promising unreleased" model from v1 (Decipher-MR, PRISM, SKELEX) is now published as a paper but still has no public weights - that pattern has not broken. The four prior RSNA Kaggle competitions remain 4-for-4 ImageNet-only at the backbone level. Net recommendation: treat backbones as a diversity lever for the ensemble, not a solo-accuracy lever, and prioritize RadioDINO and DINOv3-ConvNeXt as cheap, ungated, licensing-clean tests before touching anything gated or research-only.

## 2. Top 5 recommendations, ranked

### #1 - RadioDINO-b16 (`Snarcy/RadioDino-b16` via timm), fine-tuned, 224px native (test at your 512px too)

**Why it's likely decorrelated, not just accurate**: it is DINO self-supervised pretraining (not the supervised classification objective your RadImageNet ResNet-50 would use) on RadImageNet's 1.35M CT/MRI/ultrasound images spanning 11 anatomies including knee. That combines two decorrelation sources at once versus your ConvNeXt lineup: different pretraining objective (SSL vs. supervised ImageNet-22k) AND different pretraining domain (radiology vs. natural images). It is also architecturally a ViT, while your whole current stack is ConvNeXt - a third source of decorrelation in the ensemble.
**Concrete experiment**: `timm.create_model("hf_hub:Snarcy/RadioDino-b16", pretrained=True)`, fine-tune end-to-end on your 96-slice pipeline. Confirmed to load via timm directly - no custom code. License CC-BY-4.0, ungated.
**Expected cost**: comparable to your existing single-backbone runs (same 96-slice pipeline, ViT-B is ~86M params, similar to ConvNeXt-Base). ~$15-25 on Modal H200.
**Risk**: low on access/license; medium on whether ViT-B at native 224px transfers well when your pipeline runs at 512px (verify the model accepts non-native resolution via timm's dynamic `img_size` interpolation, which ViT backbones generally support).

### #2 - DINOv3-distilled ConvNeXt-Base (`timm/convnext_base.dinov3_lvd1689m`), fine-tuned, 512px

**Why it's likely decorrelated**: same architecture family as your current best models (ConvNeXt), but the *pretraining* is DINOv3 self-supervised distillation from a 7B-parameter teacher on 1.7B natural images (LVD-1689M), not supervised ImageNet-22k classification. Same backbone shape, different feature statistics from a fundamentally different training signal - this is close to a controlled experiment isolating "does the pretraining recipe matter, holding architecture fixed." It is nearly a drop-in swap of your existing ConvNeXt-Base training script.
**Concrete experiment**: swap `timm.create_model('convnext_base', pretrained=True)` for `timm.create_model('convnext_base.dinov3_lvd1689m', pretrained=True)` at 512px (matches your current resolution). Fully fine-tune, don't freeze.
**Expected cost**: identical to a normal ConvNeXt-Base run, ~$15-20.
**License caveat you must resolve before shipping**: DINOv3 now permits commercial use but under a **custom Meta license**, not Apache-2.0 - it requires displaying "Built with DINOv3" and requires that if you redistribute the weights (e.g., inside your Kaggle submission dataset) you pass along the same license terms. Confirm this is compatible with the competition's redistribution/dataset rules before relying on it for a prize submission.
**Risk**: low technically, low-to-medium on license administrivia (readable, not gated behind an approval queue, but requires the attribution).

### #3 - Curia-2 (`raidium/curia-2`) purely as a research/EDA probe, NOT a submission candidate

**Why it's interesting despite the license**: Curia-2 (released within the last two weeks as of this research) is a DINOv2-based ViT-B (86.1M params) trained on 150,000 real hospital CT/MRI exams - the closest thing to "MRI-CORE but newer and from a paper with actual external validation." If you want a fast, informal signal on whether medical-pretrained MRI features help your task at all (before investing engineering time in a submission-legal alternative), this is the highest-quality checkpoint to probe with.
**Hard blocker**: license is RESEARCH-ONLY RAIL-M, explicitly "not intended for use in clinical decision-making" - not usable in a for-prize Kaggle submission. Use it only offline to decide whether "medical MRI SSL pretraining" is worth chasing further (e.g., via your own continued-pretraining route, recommendation below), then discard it before submission.
**Cost**: ~$15 to probe, zero risk since it never ships.

### #4 - RadImageNet ResNet-50 (unchanged from v1, still worth running)

Still the only checkpoint with direct, on-point published knee-MRI transfer numbers (ACL tear +4.8% AUROC, meniscal tear +4.5% AUROC vs. ImageNet, on MRNet). Weaker architecture (ResNet-50 vs. your ConvNeXt/ViT), so unlikely to win solo, but it is supervised on a completely different objective (165-class radiology pathology classification) from everything else on your list, making it a strong low-cost diversity pick. `convergedmachine/RadImagenet` weights load as native timm state dicts. MIT/Apache-2.0, ungated. ~$15.

### #5 - Continued self-supervised pretraining on your own ~350k unlabeled knee-MRI slices, via LightlyTrain or the DINOv2 reference repo

Unchanged core logic from v1, still the most theoretically sound lever (matches your exact data distribution, zero licensing risk, you own the checkpoint), but now with a lower-friction path: **LightlyTrain** (`docs.lightly.ai`) packages DINOv2-style distillation pretraining as a library call rather than requiring you to wrangle Meta's multi-node reference DINOv2 codebase, which several GitHub issues confirm is awkward to run on a single node/GPU for a custom dataset. Practical estimate for a useful (not SOTA) continued-pretraining run on ~350k images, single H200: on the order of a few GPU-hours to a day (community reports of small-dataset DINOv2-style continued pretraining completing in well under 24 GPU-hours; note the original DINOv2 ImageNet-22k-from-scratch run took ~3.3 days on 12 nodes, but that is training from nothing on a much larger corpus - continued pretraining initialized from an existing checkpoint on a smaller, narrower corpus is far cheaper). Risk: medium (no guarantee of gain, needs the most engineering time of anything on this list). Expected gain if it works: plausibly the largest single number on this list, because it is the only option that directly targets your exact data distribution.

## 4. Prior RSNA competition winning solutions

Re-confirmed and slightly extended from v1. All prior RSNA Kaggle competitions used ImageNet-pretrained CNN backbones, never medical-pretrained ones, at the per-slice encoder level:

- **RSNA 2019 Intracranial Hemorrhage** (1st place): SE-ResNeXt-101, DenseNet-121/169 + a sequence model over slices. ImageNet only.
- **RSNA 2022 Cervical Spine Fracture Detection** (1st place, GitHub writeups by darraghdog and others): 2.5D CNN-RNN - a 2D backbone (EfficientNet-V2-S or ConvNeXt) encodes each slice (concatenated with neighboring slices and segmentation masks as extra channels), an LSTM fuses features across slices per vertebra. ImageNet only.
- **RSNA 2023 Abdominal Trauma Detection** (1st place, `github.com/Nischaydnk/RSNA-2023-1st-place-solution`): 2.5D slice-fusion, 2D CNN backbones (CoaT Lite / EfficientNet-V2-S), volumes split into triplets of adjacent slices, a GRU models inter-slice dependencies, auxiliary segmentation heads, max-pooling over slice logits. ImageNet only.
- **RSNA 2024 Lumbar Spine Degenerative Classification** (1st place, "Avengers" team, writeup exists at `kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/writeups/avengers-1st-place-solution` but the page content could not be extracted via automated fetch - it needs a logged-in Kaggle session, same limitation v1 hit). Other public solution repos (e.g. `DaoyuanLi2816`'s silver-medal writeup, `nshen7/rsna-2024`) confirm the pattern: localize-then-classify with ResNet/ConvNeXt/Swin-family ImageNet backbones on 2.5D crops.

The pattern is unbroken: 4 competitions, 0 medical-pretrained backbones among the winners. The consistent architectural lesson instead is slice-sequence aggregation (LSTM/GRU over a 2D backbone), not backbone pretraining source.

## 5. Ruled out, and why

- **Decipher-MR** - now a real, published paper (npj Digital Medicine, 2026), but weights still not released; the paper's own described usage pattern (frozen encoder + external decoder) implies no public checkpoint exists to freeze.
- **PRISM** - unverified weight release, unchanged from v1.
- **SKELEX** - now published (Feb 2026) but weights not found; also wrong modality (X-ray, not MRI) even if released.
- **OrthoDiffusion** - the single best-matched pretraining domain in this whole report (real knee MRI, SSL, right anatomy) but (a) no confirmed weight release, data is private, and (b) it's a 3D diffusion architecture, architecturally incompatible with your per-slice 2D mean-pool encoder pipeline even if weights appeared.
- **Curia / Curia-2** - downloadable and ungated, but RESEARCH-ONLY RAIL-M license explicitly forbids clinical/commercial use - a real hazard for a prize Kaggle competition. Usable only as an internal research probe, never for a submission.
- **MRI-CORE** - best theoretical modality/anatomy fit after OrthoDiffusion (2D, MRI-only, huge, permissive CC-BY-4.0 license), but it's a SAM-style segmentation encoder with an unofficial Google Drive checkpoint link that was not test-downloaded, and abstract-level claims of classification gains could not be backed with extractable numbers.
- **Merlin** - real, ungated, Nature-published CT foundation model, but wrong modality (CT, not MRI) and 3D architecture. Ruled out on both counts.
- **MedImageInsight** - official access route is Azure Foundry, not a plain weight download; while Microsoft's Foundry policy implies MIT licensing, the community HF mirror's exact provenance and permanence were not independently re-verified in this pass.
- **RAD-DINO, BiomedCLIP, MedSigLIP/MedGemma** - unchanged from v1: RAD-DINO is chest-X-ray-only (wrong modality), MedSigLIP/MedGemma remain gated behind Google's HAI-DEF terms with unresolved redistribution rights, BiomedCLIP's PMC figure-caption pretraining is a weak proxy for clinical MRI slices.
- **DINOv3 ViT variants (non-ConvNeXt)** - technically available and license-clarified, but not prioritized here because your pipeline is already ConvNeXt-shaped; the ConvNeXt-distilled DINOv3 variant (recommendation #2) captures the same pretraining benefit with a much smaller architecture-mismatch risk.

## 6. What changed since the v1 research doc

- **DINOv3 licensing clarified**: v1 flagged it as an open licensing question. It is now confirmed: Meta permits commercial use under a custom license (not Apache-2.0), requiring a "Built with DINOv3" attribution and pass-through terms on redistribution. This resolves v1's uncertainty enough to justify actually running the experiment, contingent on your team confirming the redistribution clause is compatible with shipping weights inside a Kaggle Dataset for a prize submission.
- **New candidate found that v1 missed entirely**: RadioDINO (`Snarci/Radio-DINO`, HF `Snarcy/RadioDino-b16`/`-s8`). This applies DINO self-supervised training directly on top of RadImageNet's medical images, and is confirmed loadable with a single `timm.create_model("hf_hub:...")` call - the easiest-to-integrate medical-domain-adjacent backbone in the entire report, and cleanly licensed (CC-BY-4.0, ungated).
- **Curia-2 released**: a newer, better version of Curia now exists (raidium/curia-2, released within roughly the last two weeks of this research), but it carries forward the same RESEARCH-ONLY RAIL-M license hazard v1 flagged for the original Curia - the licensing verdict is unchanged, only the checkpoint quality claim has improved.
- **Decipher-MR and SKELEX moved from preprint to published paper**, but in both cases weights remain unreleased - v1's "not released" verdict holds for both, now with higher confidence since publication normally increases the odds of a companion code/weight release, and none appeared.
- **OrthoDiffusion is a genuinely new find** (arXiv 2602.20752, Feb 2026) not present in v1 at all - the first model in either report trained via self-supervision directly on knee MRI specifically. It doesn't change the practical recommendation because weights are not released and the architecture (3D diffusion) doesn't fit your pipeline, but it is worth monitoring: if this group later releases an encoder checkpoint, it would be the single best-matched option found across both reports.
- **MRI-CORE's own claims shifted**: v1 found only segmentation evaluations; the current abstract now explicitly claims "classification" improvements too, though I could not extract numbers to substantiate this - flagged as a claim upgrade without full verification.
- **v1's core strategic conclusion is unchanged and reinforced**: prior RSNA Kaggle winners still show 0-for-4 medical-pretrained backbones at the encoder level: this v2 pass added confirmation for RSNA 2022 and re-confirmed 2023, strengthening rather than overturning that conclusion.
