# Big-gain ideas research (target: +0.01 or more)

Date: 2026-10-06. Scope: past RSNA/Kaggle winners, noisy-label learning, knee MRI papers, semi-supervised/transductive, 3D/multi-view, AUROC tricks, distillation. Every claim has a URL. Evidence strength is rated honestly (strong / medium / weak / anecdotal).

## 0. Executive summary

1. Nothing in the literature gives a guaranteed +0.01 on a plateaued, correlated ensemble. The honest read: the live competition itself says the binding constraint is LABELS, not architecture (see section 1). Our own state (members correlated after teacher blending) matches what top Kagglers report.
2. The most credible +0.01 levers are label-side: (a) more diverse, better-handled weak label sources (silence handling per finding, several LLMs/prompts), (b) a principled report+image label fusion replacing the fixed 50/50 blend, (c) image-grounded relabelling of the disagreement set.
3. Architecture levers with evidence: a different model FAMILY for decorrelation (a public thread reports a fine-tuned Qwen 3.5 2B VLM at 0.950 single fold), not bigger encoders (a public ablation shows 4x encoder = +0.0011, a null).
4. Cheap knobs worth one run each: Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), noise-robust losses (SCE/ELR).
5. Weak evidence for big gain: AUC-maximisation losses, 3D/video models, test-time training, more TTA, rank-weight tuning.

## 3. Idea catalogue (each with evidence, fit, cost, risk)

### A. Diverse, better-handled weak-label sources (RANK 1)
- What: build several independent label sets (different LLMs, different prompts, one with gold few-shot examples, one local open model), keep "not addressed" as a first-class state, treat it per finding (silence = absent for Baker's/OA/MCL; silence = missing/mask for Synovitis/PF OA/Fracture/Effusion-related). Train one teacher per label source, then pseudo-label and train final models on blends. Different label sources make members decorrelated, which our ensemble currently lacks.
- Evidence: medium-anecdotal. Label work moved a public single model 0.938 to 0.949 (thread 735304). Synovitis fill from Effusion lifted the label key 0.878 to 0.887 (thread 733932). Few-shot gold examples in prompt: LB +0.002 (thread 745861). A team's external-model arm correlated 0.83 with theirs and gave +0.004 public (thread 735304).
- Why for us: our weakest findings (Synovitis 0.87, Lateral OA, Lateral Meniscus) are exactly the ones where report silence or wording is the issue (Synovitis 84% silent, Lateral OA 10 of 11 positives mentioned but image model below report target).
- Cost: low to medium (API under 5 USD per set; teachers are existing recipes). Risk: 58-study ruler cannot rank label sets (SE 0.04 to 0.09 per finding); a better key was not automatically a better model in 2 independent reports (threads 733932, 740610). Use image-model audit, not key AUC, as the judge.

### B. Probabilistic report+image label fusion (RANK 2)
- What: replace the fixed 50% report + 50% teacher blend with a per-finding model of how reliable the report label is, as a function of report state (explicit positive, explicit negative, silent, uncertain) and the image-teacher probability. Classic "learning from crowds": treat report-label and each teacher as annotators with confusion matrices, estimate by EM, optionally anchor with the 58 expert labels (as a few extra EM observations or priors, not as a fitting target). Output posterior soft labels.
- Evidence: Raykar et al. 2010 (jointly learn classifier and annotator quality, beats majority vote) https://jmlr.org/beta/papers/v11/raykar10a.html ; VisualCheXbert shows report labels disagree systematically with image labels and a calibrated mapping beats raw report labels by +0.14 mean F1 https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11467 ; PANDA label cleaning (section 2); meta-label-correction family https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.03809 and small-clean-set meta-learning https://arxiv.org/html/2010.06939v2 . Evidence for OUR setting: weak to medium (principled and cheap, no direct competition proof).
- Why for us: per-finding image-vs-report AUC (thread 740610) shows opposite signs per finding, so one global 0.5 is wrong for some findings and right for others.
- Cost: low (a script on existing OOF outputs, then a normal retrain). Risk: overfitting the 58; mitigate with leave-one-out across the 58 and cap parameter count (2 per finding per state).

### C. Image-grounded relabelling of the disagreement set with a vision-language model (RANK 3)
- What: where report label and OOF teacher disagree strongly (maybe 5 to 10% of cells, concentrated in weak findings), send report text PLUS the specific crop slices (use the CAM joint crop, plus the 3 most informative slices per plane) to a hosted multimodal LLM and ask for a graded verdict; use as a third annotator inside idea B (not as ground truth).
- Evidence: anecdotal. One user got 0.942 single model with Claude text labels and found few-shot examples helped LB +0.002; another measured only 80 to 90% accuracy when images were included and said it is not good enough as pure pseudo-label. Active labelling idea endorsed in thread 745861. MedGemma probing did not help (same thread). Radiologists themselves disagree with report labels (VisualCheXbert above), so a third voice on only the disputed cells is the lowest-risk use.
- Cost: medium (API spend; need slice selection logic). Risk: VLMs are weak on MRI; if used as hard labels it will inject noise. Smoke-test one call, measure agreement with the 58 on disputed cells only.

### D. Noise-robust optimisation: SAM/ASAM, SCE, ELR (RANK 4)
- What: SAM optimiser (one extra forward/backward) as a config knob; symmetric cross entropy (SCE) or ELR on top of the soft-target BCE.
- Evidence: SAM is reported robust to label noise by tens of points on CIFAR with synthetic noise, comparable to specialised methods https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.03676 , https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.11600 . On REAL medical noise a 2025 benchmark found regularisation losses (SCE, CDR) best and most sample-selection methods WORSE than plain cross entropy, gains modest (a few points of accuracy, ResNet-50) https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09315v1 . ELR and its multi-label variant https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.00151 . Evidence for +0.01 macro AUROC: weak. Realistic: +0.002 to +0.005.
- Cost: low (2x step time for SAM). Risk: our soft-blend labels are already denoised; fp16 plus SAM instabilities.

### E. A different model family for decorrelation (RANK 5)
- What: add one genuinely different member: (i) jointly fine-tuned VLM vision tower with LoRA (Qwen 3.5 2B reported 0.950 single fold, thread 735304; check weight licence on its Hugging Face card, Qwen small models are typically Apache 2.0 but verify), or (ii) frozen strong backbone plus lightweight multi-view plugin head (AnyMC3D: about 1M parameters per task on one frozen 2D backbone, multi-view input, won VLM3D challenge, 2D beats 3D architectures for 3D classification, CC BY 4.0 paper) https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12887 .
- Evidence: medium-anecdotal for (i); medium for (ii) as a design (win in a different challenge). Public team: external arm with correlation 0.83 gave +0.004 (thread 735304).
- Why for us: our members are correlated because they share labels and input pipeline; a different input handling (VLM sees 4 views as multi-image sequence, cross-view attention for free) changes error structure.
- Cost: high (training a 2B VLM on 8 GB local not possible; Modal needed; inference 2x T4 within 9 h should be fine for 2B at 384 px but must be timed). Risk: fp16 on T4 (no bf16), long context for many images; CoPAS-like cross-plane attention needs custom code.

### F. Dense report-derived auxiliary targets (RANK 6)
- What: extra heads trained on structured attributes read from the reports by the LLM (ACL partial vs complete, meniscus horn/tear type, compartment, cartilage grade, bone marrow edema location, joint space narrowing, osteophytes), plus "atomic" rubric Q and A as proposed in thread 745861. These tie weak findings (Lateral OA, Lateral Meniscus, PF OA) to finer visual concepts.
- Evidence: medium for auxiliary dense supervision in general (RSNA 2023 1st: aux seg losses "+0.01 to +0.03" https://github.com/Nischaydnk/RSNA-2023-1st-place-solution ; 3rd lumbar: aux losses improved; CoPAS multi-task helps low-data findings https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11368947 ). Caution: a 2025 study found that when the diagnostic label is already highly predictable from text, distilling report text into the image model gives no benefit over image-only https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24385 . Our labels ARE report-derived, so only attributes that go BEYOND the 12 labels can add signal.
- Cost: medium (one more LLM pass over 4.4k reports, new heads). Risk: attribute labels equally noisy; expected gain +0.002 to +0.006.

### G. Transductive use of the hidden test set (RANK 7)
- What: inside the notebook, (1) kNN/label-propagation smoothing of predictions in embedding space across the ~1300 test studies, (2) one round of head-only pseudo-label fine-tune on test embeddings (cheap, minutes), (3) per-finding rank normalisation across the test set.
- Evidence: weak. 3rd place lumbar: pseudo-labels from unlabeled data "not significant" https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/539453 . Noisy Student strong on ImageNet but with 300M unlabeled images https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.04252 ; we have 1300 and noisy ones. Test-time adaptation literature targets distribution shift, not label noise https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03133 . Rank-normalising over the test set is already implicit in AUROC.
- Cost: low to medium. Risk: cannot be validated except on 58 and the public LB; can hurt silently. Only worth it if the hidden set differs in site mix from train.

### H. Distillation of the ensemble into fast students (RANK 8, efficiency prize plus freed budget)
- What: function-matching distillation (same augmented view fed to teacher and student, long schedule), per Beyer et al. "patient and consistent" https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05237 . Not an AUROC gain by itself; teacher blend is already a form of distillation.
- Value: the competition has an efficiency award (mentioned in the prize description https://www.rsna.org/news/2026/august/ai-challenge-knee-mri and an efficiency leaderboard exists), and a student frees inference time for heavier TTA or an extra family member. Public reports: single ResNet-50 5-fold 0.954, tiny models at 0.93 to 0.94 (thread 735304), so a small distilled student is plausible.
- Cost: medium. Risk: zero for score if the ensemble is kept for the main submission.

### Lower-priority ideas (evidence summary)
- AUC-maximisation losses (LibAUC AUCM): won CheXpert 2020 and beats CE on imbalanced medical tasks in the authors' studies https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03173 ; but with soft noisy targets and 12 heads the pairwise loss amplifies label noise; evidence for our case weak. One test only if cheap (LibAUC has multi-label CheXpert example https://github.com/Optimization-AI/LibAUC/blob/main/examples/scripts/07_optimizing_multi_label_auroc_loss_with_densenet121_on_chexpert.py ). One public user mixed BCE with a pairwise ranking loss in one DINO arm (thread 740610), no isolated gain reported.
- 3D / video models: AnyMC3D finds 2D foundation models plus plugins beat 3D architectures; RSNA winners all used 2.5D. Skip.
- Cross-plane attention: CoPAS (three plane branches, cross-plane attention, plane-aware fusion, multi-task) improved knee average AUC 0.781 to 0.812 over MRNet on a 12-abnormality set, strongest on weaker findings https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11368947 ; our mean pooling over views probably leaves something here, and idea E (VLM) gets cross-view attention implicitly. Medium evidence, moderate coding cost. A synthetic coronal view by rotating volumes was used there to offset anisotropy.
- Knee-specific papers and foundation models: ELNet adds only +0.004 AUC over MRNet https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.02706 ; KAMRNet 9-abnormality multi-task system (code https://github.com/zqiuak/KAMRNet, paper https://www.thelancet.com/journals/eclinm/article/PIIS2589-5370(25)00467-5/fulltext ; page not retrievable so details unread); OrthoDiffusion (3 orientation-specific 3D diffusion models, 15,948 unlabeled knee MRI, 8 abnormalities, strong with 10% labels, CC BY 4.0 paper, weight availability and licence not verified) https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20752 ; MRI-CORE (already used) CC BY 4.0 https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12186v1 ; MedSigLIP/MedGemma are free for commercial use under Google HAI-DEF terms but a participant found MedGemma did not help https://developers.google.com/health-ai-developer-foundations/medsiglip . None of these is a likely +0.01 given the encoder-scaling null.
- Self-supervised pretraining helps robustness to noisy labels in medical imaging https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.04551 ; continued in-domain DINO pretraining on our 4.4k studies is heavy and the encoder-size null suggests representation is not binding. Skip unless idea A to C stall.
- Late label cleaning (drop worst-disagreeing studies) is a documented win in lumbar 2nd and PANDA; our soft blend subsumes it, but a hard drop of the top 2 to 3% disputed cells per weak finding at the final fit is a free, cheap check.
- Soft positive labels / label smoothing 0.1 to 0.2: small, standard.
- Checkpoint-contract hygiene: read mean/std from each checkpoint's preprocessor config (RAD-DINO case, thread 735154).

## 5. Caveats

- Several Kaggle sources are forum posts with unverifiable numbers (public LB can be probed; others report the public board "will lie"). Treat all anecdotal gains as hypotheses.
- Kaggle write-ups for RSNA 2024 1st (Avengers), RSNA 2022 details and KAMRNet could not be read in full (pages not retrievable); the RSNA 2024 2nd/3rd, RSNA 2022 1st, RSNA 2023 1st, breast 1st content above was read directly.
- The 58-study panel cannot rank small effects (per-finding SE 0.04 to 0.09, thread 735154); decide on consistency across audit-58, validation split and ensemble-gain from cached predictions, as our own notes already do.
- Licence checks still owed before shipping anything new: Qwen vision tower weights (Hugging Face card), OrthoDiffusion weights, any local LLM used for labels (labels themselves are fine, weights not shipped).
