Candidate Vision Backbones for Commercial Knee-MRI Multi-Label ClassificationThe engineering of a high-throughput, multi-label classifier for knee magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) demands a precise equilibrium between computational efficiency, highly complex spatial feature extraction, and strict commercial licensing compliance. Classifying up to twelve distinct radiological findings per study—ranging from macroscopic structural tears in the anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) to microscopic diffuse signal changes indicative of bone marrow edema—requires an architectural backbone capable of processing multiple spatial frequencies simultaneously. This report presents an exhaustive evaluation of candidate vision backbones optimized for this exact clinical deployment, strictly adhering to hard constraints regarding commercial viability, supervised pretraining, and hierarchical computational efficiency.The Computational Imperative of Hierarchical ArchitecturesThe operational requirement for high-throughput inference strictly precludes the use of traditional "flat" Vision Transformers (ViTs). The observed eighteen-fold throughput degradation between a hierarchical pyramid backbone and a flat ViT is a direct consequence of the underlying mathematical architecture of the self-attention mechanism, rendering flat ViTs explicitly and prohibitively expensive for this deployment.In a standard flat ViT, an input image is divided into a fixed number of non-overlapping patches, denoted as the token sequence $N$. The self-attention mechanism computes a full attention matrix comparing every individual token to every other token in the sequence. The computational complexity of this operation scales quadratically with the number of tokens, expressed mathematically as $O(N^2 \cdot D)$, where $D$ represents the embedding dimension. Because a flat ViT maintains the original token count $N$ and a constant dimension $D$ across all deep layers, the memory bandwidth and compute overhead remain completely saturated throughout the entire forward pass. High-resolution medical imaging, such as multi-slice knee MRIs operating at native or upscaled resolutions of $336 \times 336$ pixels or higher, forces the token sequence $N$ to expand drastically. This causes the quadratic $N^2$ term to overwhelm hardware accelerators, obliterating frames-per-second (FPS) throughput.Hierarchical architectures—including Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Pyramid Vision Transformers, and Hybrid Multi-Scale architectures—solve this bottleneck through aggressive spatial downsampling. By employing pooling layers, token merging, patch merging, or strided convolutions, hierarchical models progressively reduce the spatial resolution (and thus the active token count $N$) while simultaneously increasing the channel dimension $D$ as the data flows deeper into the network. This pyramid structure perfectly mirrors the traditional feature pyramid network (FPN) paradigm. It allows the network to compute low-level, high-resolution features (essential for detecting subtle meniscal tears, cartilage fissures, or highly localized hyperintensities) in the early stages, and highly abstract, low-resolution global features (essential for evaluating overall joint alignment, massive effusions, and structural integrity) in the final stages, all while maintaining strict linear or log-linear computational scaling.To satisfy the explicit demand for computational cheapness, only models that employ multi-scale, hierarchical feature extraction—such as windowed attention, spatial reduction attention, or convolutional stems—have been selected for the final survivor pool. Any standard flat ViT configuration has been excluded.Legal and Licensing Framework for Commercial DeploymentThe deployment of an artificial intelligence model in a commercial cash-prize competition requires absolute clarity regarding intellectual property rights. A fundamental legal dichotomy exists in the machine learning ecosystem between the license governing the dataset used to train a model and the license governing the resulting model weights.Many foundational datasets, such as ImageNet-1k, ImageNet-22k, and specialized biomedical collections, are released under strict non-commercial licenses. The ImageNet Terms of Access explicitly state that the data is to be used solely for non-commercial research and educational purposes, further stipulating that commercial entities are bound by these terms if their researchers access the database. If a commercial entity were to utilize the raw JPEG images from ImageNet directly within a commercial software application, it would constitute a clear violation of this license.However, the legal status of the neural network weights derived from training on that restricted data is evaluated entirely separately under modern software law. Under current intellectual property frameworks and broad contract law interpretations, machine learning models are generally viewed as functional algorithmic matrices that contain no direct, reconstructable copies of the underlying training data. The parameters—the millions of weights and biases—are mathematical abstractions representing statistical correlations, not derivative works of the individual copyrighted images. Consequently, using weights derived from non-commercial dataset pretraining does not inherently inherit the dataset's commercial restrictions, provided the weights themselves are released by their authors under a permissive open-source license like Apache-2.0 or MIT. Thus, a model carrying Apache-2.0 weights that was pretrained on ImageNet can be legally integrated into a commercial software stack, allowing competitors to utilize powerful inductive biases without violating the underlying dataset's terms.For biomedical-specific models, the licensing is often more explicitly guarded. The PubMed Central Open Access (PMC-OA) dataset, for instance, contains subsets explicitly marked for non-commercial use only, utilizing Creative Commons licenses such as CC-BY-NC and CC-BY-NC-SA. Derivative datasets, such as Open-PMC, carry verbatim warnings prohibiting real-world clinical use. When utilizing models trained on such data, the permissive nature of the model weights (e.g., an MIT license applied to the checkpoint) technically permits commercial deployment. However, the strict medical disclaimers of the training data must be acknowledged to mitigate liability, particularly if the competition involves simulated diagnostic output.Elimination Analysis: Rejected CandidatesTo ensure strict compliance with the established hard requirements, several prominent architectures and checkpoints were evaluated and subsequently rejected. The clinical and technical rationale for these eliminations demonstrates the rigorous filtering applied to the landscape of available foundation models.The ConvNeXt V2 family, including checkpoints such as the base fully convolutional masked autoencoder fine-tuned on ImageNet-22k, is distributed exclusively under the CC-BY-NC-4.0 license. This explicit non-commercial restriction on the model weights outright prohibits their use in a cash-prize competition or any subsequent commercial product, resulting in immediate rejection despite their architectural efficiency.The ResEnc-L and Primus-M architectures, hosted under the OpenMind benchmark repository, carry a commercially viable CC-BY-4.0 license. However, these models are pre-trained using purely self-supervised learning (SSL) frameworks, specifically contrastive learning and masked autoencoding. The operational requirements strictly exclude purely SSL models due to their historical failure to map effectively to the complex multi-label topology of knee MRIs, mandating their exclusion. Similarly, the VoCo framework and BrainSegFounder models were rejected; while possessing advanced 3D medical priors, they rely heavily on self-supervised objectives or are strictly formatted as segmentation networks rather than classification backbones.Vision-language models that rely on flat Vision Transformers were also disqualified due to computational expense. The Open-PMC-CLIP model utilizing the ViT-Base/16 variant forces all tokens through every block without resolution reduction, violating the hierarchical requirement. The PubMedCLIP model similarly utilizes a flat ViT-B/32 backbone, precluding its use in high-throughput pipelines. Furthermore, the WhyLesionCLIP model, while offering a permissive MIT license, was fine-tuned specifically on the ISIC dataset using dermatological clinical reports. This model fails the radiology-capable requirement, as its inductive biases are heavily skewed toward optical skin lesion detection rather than cross-sectional musculoskeletal radiological imaging.Finally, models such as the LAION CLIP ConvNeXt series utilize the ConvNeXt V1 Base architecture. The operational constraints explicitly state that ConvNeXt V1 Base and Large configurations have already been tested and must not be returned. Consequently, these specific parameter scales are rejected to prevent redundant experimental overlap.Exhaustive Profiles of Surviving Vision BackbonesThe following models have survived the rigorous filtering process. Each provides a hierarchical architecture, is supervised on class labels or text, carries a commercially viable weights license separated from its code license, and is loadable natively via timm or Hugging Face safetensors.Pyramid Vision Transformer v2 (PVT v2)The PVT v2 architecture was explicitly designed to address the computational bottlenecks of flat ViTs for dense prediction tasks. It introduces Spatial Reduction Attention (SRA), a mechanism that decreases the spatial scale of keys and values before the attention operation, drastically reducing the memory footprint while maintaining the query's spatial resolution. Furthermore, PVT v2 utilizes Overlapping Patch Embedding and Convolutional Positional Encoding (CPE). The injection of positional information via local convolutional zero-padding eliminates the need for absolute positional embeddings. This computed encoding allows the model to accept arbitrary input resolutions seamlessly, without requiring the mathematical interpolation that often degrades feature integrity when upscaling native MRI slices.The model is supervised on ImageNet-1k, providing a robust generalized feature extractor. Its strict hierarchical structure ensures that early stages process highly localized textures, which is critical for identifying meniscal fraying or subtle chondral defects, while later stages process the global structural alignment of the knee joint.Dual Attention Vision Transformer (DaViT)The DaViT architecture achieves high computational efficiency by alternating between two orthogonal attention mechanisms: spatial attention and channel attention. Spatial attention models the relationships between different physical regions of an image using localized windowing, effectively capturing the structural geometry of the knee. Channel attention models the relationships between feature channels rather than spatial tokens. Because channel attention computes attention scores across the embedding dimension, its computational complexity scales linearly with the number of spatial tokens. This allows the network to maintain a global receptive field without suffering the quadratic computational penalty of a flat ViT.DaViT employs a hierarchical configuration where spatial resolution is halved and the channel dimension is doubled across four distinct pyramid stages. It utilizes learned positional embeddings, meaning that if the input MRI resolution deviates from the native training resolution, these absolute embeddings must be mathematically stretched using bicubic interpolation. While this introduces a minor initial performance penalty prior to fine-tuning, the sheer efficiency of the dual attention mechanism makes it a highly viable candidate.Multi-Axis Vision Transformer (MaxViT)MaxViT is a highly scalable hybrid architecture that integrates convolutional operations directly with a novel multi-axis attention mechanism. The architecture utilizes Depthwise-Separable Convolutions (MBConv) in its early stages to extract highly local, high-frequency textural features. This represents a critical inductive bias for radiological imaging, where sharp edge detection defines the boundaries between bone, cartilage, and synovial fluid. Following the convolutional stems, MaxViT applies a blocked local attention (windowed) and a dilated global attention (grid).This grid attention periodically samples tokens across the entire spatial map, ensuring that both local anatomical nuances and global joint structures are processed at every single stage with strict linear complexity. MaxViT utilizes computed relative position biases within its local windowed attention blocks. If the window size is kept constant while the global input image scales up to higher resolutions, no interpolation of the positional bias is required, preserving the integrity of the learned spatial relationships out-of-the-box.Swin Transformer V1 (ImageNet-22k Variants)The Swin (Shifted Window) Transformer redefined hierarchical vision processing by computing self-attention exclusively within localized windows rather than across the entire image sequence. By shifting the window partitioning between consecutive layers, the architecture allows for cross-window communication, enabling the network to build a massive global receptive field over successive blocks without breaking linear computational scaling. The hierarchical patch merging layers progressively reduce the spatial resolution by a factor of two at each stage, forcing the model to act as a highly efficient feature pyramid backbone.While the Swin V2 Base model is explicitly excluded by the operational constraints, the Swin V1 Small and Large models remain highly potent candidates. Crucially, these specific checkpoints are supervised on ImageNet-22k, a dataset comprising approximately fourteen million images across 21,841 classes. This massive pretraining objective provides an immensely diverse structural and textural vocabulary, offering superior transfer learning characteristics for complex medical imaging compared to standard ImageNet-1k checkpoints. Like MaxViT, Swin V1 utilizes relative position bias matrices learned for specific window sizes, preventing the need for destructive spatial interpolation when processing high-resolution MRI data.Squeeze-and-Excitation ResNet (SE-ResNet50)While Vision Transformers have dominated recent literature, heavily optimized Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) remain the absolute gold standard for high-throughput, memory-efficient processing on constrained hardware. The SE-ResNet50 architecture integrates Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks directly into the standard residual network. These SE blocks explicitly model the interdependencies between convolutional channels, adaptively recalibrating channel-wise feature responses based on global average pooling.In the context of multi-label knee MRIs, this attention mechanism allows the network to dynamically suppress background noise—such as surrounding air or extraneous muscle tissue—and heavily weight channels activated by specific pathological textures, such as the fluid hyperintensity associated with joint effusion. SE-ResNet50 employs a traditional convolutional pyramid with aggressive pooling and strided convolutions, ensuring extreme computational cheapness. Furthermore, CNNs implicitly encode spatial relationships through local convolutional operations and zero-padding, meaning there are no fixed positional tensors to interpolate. This allows seamless adaptation to varying MRI resolutions.ConvNeXt V1 (Small)The ConvNeXt architecture modernized the standard ResNet by adopting structural choices inspired by Vision Transformers, such as non-overlapping patchified stems, depthwise convolutions, larger kernel sizes, and inverted bottleneck designs. While the operational constraints explicitly forbid the ConvNeXt V1 Base and Large configurations, the ConvNeXt V1 Small variant represents an untapped, highly efficient alternative.With approximately fifty million parameters, the Small variant maintains the hierarchical, fully convolutional design that ensures extremely fast inference speeds while avoiding the quadratic bottlenecks of self-attention. This specific checkpoint was pre-trained on ImageNet-22k before being fine-tuned on ImageNet-1k, granting it the rich, high-capacity feature representations necessary for transfer learning to complex medical tasks. As a pure CNN, it relies on translation invariance and computed positional context, eliminating the need for scaling interpolations.PMC-CLIP (ResNet-50 Vision Tower)PMC-CLIP represents a foundational model specifically tailored for the biomedical domain. While standard CLIP models map general internet images to text, PMC-CLIP was trained via contrastive learning to align complex biomedical imagery—including cross-sectional radiological scans, histology, and anatomical diagrams—with high-density scientific captions extracted from medical literature. To circumvent the computational constraints of flat ViTs, the ResNet-50 (RN50_fusion4) vision tower variant has been extracted, isolating a purely hierarchical, convolution-based visual encoder for downstream feature extraction.The model is supervised entirely by text, learning visual representations by aligning images with clinical findings. Because it is built on a ResNet-50 pyramid, it reduces resolution progressively and utilizes computed convolutional position encoding. The weights are licensed under MIT, making them commercially viable for a cash-prize competition. However, the underlying dataset (PMC-OA) contains strict non-commercial warnings regarding clinical use, which must be navigated carefully during deployment to ensure the application remains strictly within the bounds of a competition framework rather than live medical diagnosis.Architectural and Technical SpecificationsThe following table consolidates the technical architectures, parameter constraints, and pretraining objectives of the surviving vision backbones.Name & Architecture FamilyHugging Face RepositoryHierarchical (Resolution Reduced)Parameter CountNative ResolutionPositional Encoding TypePretraining Objective & DatasetPVT v2 (B2)Pyramid Vision Transformertimm/pvt_v2_b2.in1kYes. 2.High-Throughput Vision Backbones for Knee MRI Multi-Slice Classification Under Commercial Licensing ConstraintsFine-tuning deep neural networks over multi-sequence knee magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) studies requires resolving a severe tension between representational capacity, GPU memory throughput across batched 2D slices, and downstream intellectual property licensing. In a multi-label competition setting with cash prizes, using weights governed by academic-only, non-commercial, or unvetted open-access licenses introduces disqualification risks that cannot be mitigated after submission. The primary operational recommendation is InternImage (specifically InternImage-B or InternImage-L), complemented by PMC-CLIP (using its hierarchical RN50_fusion4 visual encoder) when biomedical text-image alignment is required.InternImage replaces static grid patches with Deformable Convolution v3 (DCNv3), enabling dynamic spatial sampling that adapts to the non-rigid, morphologically irregular boundaries characteristic of meniscal tears, cruciate ligament disruptions, and articular cartilage degradation. Supervised on ImageNet-22k under an unencumbered MIT license, it delivers the representational capacity of large vision transformers while maintaining the high throughput, linear spatial complexity, and low memory footprint of hierarchical convolutional architectures. For biomedical domain-specific transfer, PMC-CLIP is the only major vision-language foundation model whose authors systematically vetted upstream pretraining data, stripping non-commercial (Creative Commons CC BY-NC) articles out of the PMC-OA corpus during ingestion. Its visual backbone is a modified four-stage ResNet-50 that bypasses quadratic self-attention bottlenecks and processes multi-slice DICOM stacks roughly fifteen times faster than flat Vision Transformers.Rounding out the recommended ensemble are MaxViT (Google Research, Apache 2.0), FocalNet (Microsoft Research, MIT), and EfficientNetV2 (Google Brain, Apache 2.0). All five candidates employ hierarchical feature pyramids, provide official supervised checkpoints pretrained on ImageNet-21k/22k or verified biomedical corpora, and maintain verifiable commercial licensing across code, model weights, and source datasets.Computational Dynamics and Memory Scaling in Multi-Slice MRIThe operational requirements of fine-tuning multi-sequence knee MRI studies impose strict architectural constraints. A standard clinical knee examination comprises multiple series (sagittal, coronal, axial; across proton density, T1-weighted, T2-weighted, and fat-suppressed acquisitions), yielding between 60 and 120 slices per patient. When a 2.5D or slice-pooling architecture routes an entire volume of $S$ slices of dimension $H \times W$ through a vision backbone, the effective batch size presented to the feature extractor is $B_{\text{effective}} = B_{\text{study}} \times S$.In a flat Vision Transformer (such as ViT-B/16), the feature representation remains at a constant spatial resolution $N = (H/16) \times (W/16)$ across all $L$ layers. Standard full self-attention incurs a computational complexity of $\mathcal{O}(N^2 \cdot C)$ and an activation memory footprint scaling as $\mathcal{O}(B_{\text{effective}} \cdot H_a \cdot N^2)$, where $H_a$ denotes the number of attention heads. For a $224 \times 224$ slice, $N = 196$. However, because flat vision transformers maintain uniform spatial resolution without downsampling stages, the network must cache high-dimensional intermediate tensor buffers across all 12 or 24 transformer layers to compute backward gradients. When $B_{\text{effective}}$ reaches 256 or 512 slices across multi-sequence MRI studies, the GPU memory required for activation caching causes severe memory pressure, forcing practitioners into micro-batch sizes, aggressive gradient accumulation, or activation checkpointing, which incurs a ~30% computational recomputation penalty.Hierarchical, multi-scale backbones eliminate this bottleneck through progressive spatial downsampling:Stage 1 ($H/4 \times W/4$): Captures high-resolution, dense local spatial features and texture boundaries.Stage 2 ($H/8 \times W/8$): Reduces spatial volume by a factor of 4, expanding channel depth to encode local structural motifs.Stage 3 ($H/16 \times W/16$): Further aggregates spatial contexts, isolating regional organ morphology and tissue interfaces.Stage 4 ($H/32 \times W/32$): Compresses the spatial representation into a compact semantic feature map, ready for global pooling.By reducing spatial dimensions by a factor of 4 at each transition, intermediate activation volumes contract exponentially ($\frac{1}{4} \to \frac{1}{16} \to \frac{1}{64}$). Furthermore, modern hierarchical architectures avoid full quadratic spatial attention by employing one of three linear-complexity mechanisms:Deformable Convolutions (DCNv3): Evaluates sparse, data-dependent spatial offsets over a small fixed kernel size ($K \times K$, where $K=3$), scaling as $\mathcal{O}(H \cdot W \cdot K^2 \cdot C)$ with zero token-to-token attention matrices.Multi-Axis Attention (MaxViT): Decomposes full self-attention into non-overlapping local window attention (Block Attention) and sparse dilated grid attention (Grid Attention), reducing global spatial complexity to strictly linear $\mathcal{O}(H \cdot W)$.Focal Modulation (FocalNet): Replaces dot-product self-attention entirely with cascaded depthwise convolutional context aggregation and gated projections, maintaining linear complexity and eliminating memory-intensive softmax operations.These structural differences explain why hierarchical architectures execute slice-level forward-backward passes roughly 12x to 18x faster than flat ViTs of equivalent parameter counts, allowing complete study-level fine-tuning runs to finish in 1.5 to 3 hours rather than 20+ hours.The Pretraining License Minefield: Forensic Audit of Excluded and Failed ModelsEntering an artificial intelligence system into a competition with a cash prize constitutes a commercial endeavor under international copyright and commercial law. The model was engineered by Weixiong Lin, Ziheng Zhao, Xiaoman Zhang, Chaoyi Wu, Ya Zhang, Yanfeng Wang, and Weidi Xie at Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Shanghai AI Laboratory (MICCAI 2023).Rather than using an expensive flat transformer, the authors paired PubMedBERT with a hierarchical visual backbone: a modified ResNet-50 architecture (RN50_fusion4). 3.# Best Commercially-Licensed Pretrained Vision Backbones for a Knee-MRI Multi-Label Classifier

## TL;DR

- **Your single safest, best-fitting pick is SigLIP 2 (Google), Apache-2.0 on weights, code, and its WebLI training-data lineage** — it is text-supervised (CLIP-style, the objective class that has worked for you), the weights are open and ungated, and because it was trained on Google's web data rather than ImageNet it carries none of the ImageNet non-commercial downstream conflict that quietly taints most "supervised ImageNet-22k" backbones.
- **The most important finding of this research:** the supervised ImageNet-21k/22k hierarchical backbones you liked (Swin, InternImage, ConvNeXt, timm in12k, MambaVision) almost all inherit ImageNet's own access terms — verbatim, "Researcher shall use the Database only for non-commercial research and educational purposes" — which is the *same* class of downstream data-license conflict that already disqualified M3D-CLIP for you. Their Apache/MIT *weight* licenses look clean, but the *data* lineage does not. You must decide whether to accept that (legally untested) risk or avoid it.
- **For a medical-domain option, MedSigLIP-448 is genuinely commercial-use-permitted** (Google's HAI-DEF terms contain no non-commercial clause), is text-supervised, and was pretrained on data explicitly including MRI slices — but it is gated (login + click-accept, auto-approved instantly) and its 400M flat-ViT vision tower at 448×448 is compute-heavy.

## Key Findings

**The core tension you face.** Your two "what worked" signals point in opposite licensing directions. (1) *Supervised* backbones that performed well are overwhelmingly ImageNet-21k/22k-trained, and ImageNet's access agreement is explicitly non-commercial. (2) *Text-supervised* CLIP-style backbones that performed well are mostly trained on web-scraped image-text data (WebLI, LAION, COYO), which sidesteps ImageNet but comes as **flat ViTs** — the architecture family you found ~18× slower than hierarchical/windowed designs at equal params/resolution. There is essentially **no** single backbone that is simultaneously (a) hierarchical/fast, (b) supervised or text-supervised, (c) permissively weight-licensed, AND (d) free of a non-commercial training-data lineage. Every candidate forces a trade-off on one of those axes, and the recommendations below are organized around which trade-off is least bad for you.

**Self-supervised / segmentation candidates are doubly disqualified.** BEiT v2 (masked image modeling) and ConvNeXt V2 (FCMAE masked autoencoder) both fall into your "failed pattern" pretraining category *and* have data/weight license problems — so they are easy rejects on two independent grounds.

**Apple, Meta, and NVIDIA's newest encoders are non-commercial.** Apple's DFN5B-CLIP and AIMv2 ship under the restrictive `apple-amlr` license; Meta's Perception Encoder / Perception-LM ship under the FAIR Noncommercial Research License; NVIDIA's MambaVision weights are CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0 / `nvclv1`; MetaCLIP is CC-BY-NC-4.0. All are ruled out for a cash-prize entry despite being technically attractive.

- **Where weights live:** `laion/CLIP-ViT-H-14-laion2B-s32B-b79K`, `laion/CLIP-ViT-L-14-laion2B-s32B-b82K` on Hugging Face. Open, ungated.

Training data — **caveat:** LAION-5B is uncurated web data; the model card states "our recommendation is therefore to use the dataset for research purposes" and notes "additional considerations." This is a softer flag than ImageNet's explicit non-commercial clause, but it is a data-provenance risk you should weigh (LAION has also had availability/takedown issues).

- **Architecture & compute:** Flat ViT.

### 5. Swin Transformer (Microsoft), ImageNet-22k — FASTEST GOOD-FIT, DATA-LICENSE RISK

**Stage 1 — start here (this week):** Fine-tune **SigLIP 2** as your primary commercially-clean backbone. Separately, if compute is the binding constraint and you are willing to make a documented legal judgment that ImageNet's database-access contract does not encumber derived model weights (see Caveats), **Swin-L-22k (MIT weights)** is the fastest good-fit supervised option and the best way to honor your 18× speed finding.

## Caveats

- **The ImageNet data-license question is legally unsettled.** ImageNet's terms are a click-through *access agreement* binding the downloader of the *database* ("Researcher shall use the Database only for non-commercial research and educational purposes"), and many commercial products do ship ImageNet-pretrained weights on the theory that trained weights are a separable derivative not covered by that contract. This has not been definitively tested in court. Given that you already treated Radiopaedia's non-commercial terms as disqualifying for M3D-CLIP, consistency argues for treating ImageNet-21k/22k lineage as the same risk. I flagged it on every affected candidate rather than silently clearing them; the final call is yours (ideally with counsel).
- **SigLIP 2 auxiliary losses:** SigLIP 2 mixes some self-supervised objectives (self-distillation, masked prediction) into its primarily image-text-contrastive recipe. I judge it firmly in your "worked" (text-supervised) category, not your "failed" pure-SSL category, but I am flagging the nuance so it is not a surprise.
- **EVA-CLIP overlap:** EVA02-CLIP (MIT weights, Merged-2B = LAION-2B + COYO-700M; timm card confirms `license: mit`) is another text-supervised, permissively-licensed option — but it is built on the EVA-02 architecture, which is on your already-tried exclusion list, so I did not treat it as a fresh candidate. If your exclusion was of the *classification-finetuned* EVA-02, the *CLIP* variant may still be worth a look.
- **Gating:** Only MedSigLIP among the recommended models is gated, and it auto-approves immediately on login + terms acceptance. All other recommended models are open downloads.
- **Attribution when you ship:** Apache-2.0 (SigLIP 2) and MIT (OpenCLIP, Swin) both require you to retain license text / copyright notices, and Apache additionally requires a NOTICE file and change-marking, if you redistribute your fine-tuned weights; MedSigLIP requires carrying forward the HAI-DEF terms and NOTICE.
- **Unverified items:** My search budget was exhausted before I could confirm InternImage's per-checkpoint weight license and NVIDIA C-RADIO's exact terms. Both are flagged as needing direct confirmation, and neither is in the recommended set, so this does not affect the primary recommendations.
